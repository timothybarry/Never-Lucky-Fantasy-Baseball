How to read it

Statcast value is the equal-weight z-score across this league's categories (barrels, xwOBA, EV90, chase, R+RBI, SB; pitchers: K, xFIP, whiff, barrel% allowed, QS).

Box-score value is the same math on official traditional stats (HR, R, RBI, SB, AVG; pitchers: W, K, SV, ERA, WHIP). The gap between them is where the edge lives.

The pink end leans toward speed-and-average hitters and closers: the box score pays for steals, batting average and saves, and Statcast doesn't count saves as skill.

Method · the honesty layer

What's measured, what's approximated, what's missing

Measured cleanly

  • From the pitch files: barrels (launch_speed_angle = 6), 90th-pct EV and barrel% allowed (balls in play only), chase% (swings at pitches outside the zone), whiff%, K (strikeout double plays included).
  • Official season lines from the public MLB Stats API, matched by MLBAM id (every player matched, all five seasons): PA, R, RBI, SB, CS, AVG, OBP, SLG, OPS, W, L, SV, ERA, WHIP, GS, IP. Traded players are one combined line.
  • Pitch-file league totals cover regular-season games only and match MLB's published home-run totals exactly: 6,105 / 5,585 / 6,776 / 2,304 / 5,944.
  • Every barrel counted is a ball in play; I checked all five seasons and found no foul balls. So the barrel trend is contact, not a tracking change. Fouls carry exit velocity in every season, and 2021 tracked about 44% more of them than 2019, so EV90 and barrel% allowed use balls in play only and league average exit velocity isn't shown.

Labeled approximations

  • xwOBA is the mean of the speed/angle estimate over balls in play (xwOBA on contact; strikeouts and walks aren't in it). xFIP is a FIP-style proxy. QS is rebuilt from outs and runs allowed in each start (runs, not earned runs).
  • 2020 had 60 games, so every opportunity threshold that season (PA, IP, pitches, batted balls, category and leaderboard minimums) is scaled by 60/162.
  • Percentiles rank against qualified player-seasons. Pooled mode ranks across all five seasons so years compare; the tradeoff is that era shifts are baked in. Within-season mode ranks against the same year only.
  • The two slates weigh volume differently (the box score is mostly counting stats), so a big positive gap often means strong rate skills in limited playing time.

Color code

Teal: skill. Rate stats that measure skill (xwOBA, whiff%, the signal score) and players whose skill beats their stats. Yellow: volume. Counting and power (barrels, EV90, HR, R, RBI, SB, K, QS, W, SV), plus champions and records. Blue: lower is better. Chase%, xFIP, barrel% allowed, ERA, WHIP. Pink: luck and cold. Stats running ahead of skill, bottom percentiles, regression risk.

Percentile shading always runs pink → grey → teal, low to high, with lower-is-better stats flipped so teal always means good.

Fixed: the stolen-base trap

  • Pitch files can't see most steals: nearly every hitter showed 0 SB. The one to three players per season with a single stray steal event got a z-score large enough to make them the #1 “signal” hitter in earlier builds (Steven Duggar in 2019, Kelvin Gutiérrez in 2021).
  • SB, R, AVG, W, SV, ERA and WHIP now come from official season lines, so stolen bases score again, in both the league table and the value model.
  • Rosters are auto-drafted per season (10 teams, 9 hitters and 7 pitchers each). The team names carry over from year to year; the rosters don't.
